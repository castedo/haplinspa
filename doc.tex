\begin{abstract}
This document mathematically defines a type of stochastic process: a \emph{Haploid
  Lineage Tree Process}. It models lines of genetic transmission via haploid cells in a population.
The motivation is to explicitly model a biological process that generates empirical genetic
  data drawn from a reproducing population of organisms.
In contrast to the Kingman $n$-coalescent, a Haploid Lineage Tree Process is not defined
  backward-in-time from a given set of haploid samples, but instead generates them
  forward-in-time, akin to a branching process.
\end{abstract}


\section{Introduction}

A \emph{stochastic process} is a mathematical model from probability theory that is
widely used in many fields of applied mathematics.
It consists of a family of time-indexed \emph{random variables}.
We seek random variables taking values that clearly model a biological phenomenon. Our
interest is in defining the type of those values, but not specifically their probabilities.
We seek a shared basis for many specialized models that do define
specific probability models, such as the Kingman $n$-coalescent.
Some motivations for such an underlying basis include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item better understanding and evaluation of the assumptions linking mathematical and
    biological models, and
  \item deriving different mathematical results under different mathematical conditions.
\end{itemize}

The random variables in the \nameref{sec:definitions}~section model gene genealogies
\cite{wakeley_coalescent_2009} per locus across the genomes of haploid cells.
After the definition,
the \nameref{sec:related-work}~section specifies the exact mathematical correspondences to
\begin{itemize}
  \item the Kingman $n$-coalescent \cite{kingman_coalescent_1982},
  \item the genealogical tree structure defined by King et al.~\cite{king_exact_2026}, and
  \item the genome Ancestral Recombination Graph (gARG) in Wong et al.~\cite{wong_general_2024}.
\end{itemize}


\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:definitions}

A \emph{haploid lineage tree process} is a \emph{stochastic
process}~\cite{ash_probability_2000, pishro_nik_2014}, which is a family of random
variables indexed by a set typically representing time.

The features required of a haploid lineage tree process are based on three givens:
\begin{enumerate}
\item an index set $\Time \subseteq \R$,
\item a measurable space of chromosomal loci, denoted $\Loci$, and
\item a measurable space of "birthplaces", denoted $\Places$.
\end{enumerate}

We define a space of potential haploids as
\[
\Haps := \Time \times \Places
\]
and a "birth" time function mapping $\Haps$ to $\R$ as
\[
  \At((t,x)) := t
\]

Depending on the application, potential haploids might describe potential organisms, gametes, or spores.


\subsection{Haploid Lineages}

A \emph{haploid lineage} represents all the haploids transmitting genetic information at a
genomic locus to a descendant haploid.

\begin{defin}
Formally, a \emph{haploid lineage} is a well-ordered set of haploids under the order of
$-\At$ (backward-in-time).
\end{defin}

An equivalent definition is that a \emph{haploid lineage} is a set $L$ of haploids such that,
under the order induced by $\At$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $L$ is totally (linearly) ordered,
\item $L$ is strictly ordered, and
\item every subset of $L$ has a maximum (newest) haploid.
\end{enumerate}

Note that the empty set is not a lineage.

The maximum (newest) haploid in a haploid lineage $L$ is denoted $\Tip(L)$.
Let $\Pred(\ell, h)$ denote $\Tip(\Lin(\ell, h) / \{h\})$, that is, the predecessor
haploid of $h$ from which genetic information was copied at locus $\ell$.

For convenience, extend $\At$ to any lineage $L$ by letting $\At(L) = \At(\Tip(L))$.

\begin{defin}
For any haploid lineage $L$, a subset $S \subset L$ is a \emph{sublineage} of $L$ iff
\[
\forall x \in L, y \in S \left( \At(x) < \At(y) \implies x \in S \right)
\mathstop
\]
\end{defin}

\begin{defin}
The \emph{ancestral sublineage} at time $t \in \Time$ of a lineage $L$ is
\[
  \left\{  h \in L : \At(h) < t \right\}
\]
\end{defin}


\begin{defin}
A \emph{haploid lineage tree} is a set of haploid lineages $T$ where for every
pair of member lineages $A, B \in T$, $A \cap B$ is either empty or a sublineage of both $A$ and
$B$.
\end{defin}

The empty set $\emptyset$ is the trivial haploid lineage tree.

\begin{defin}
Let the limit advancing to time $t \in \R \cup \{ +\infty \}$ of
a family of time-indexed trees $\{T_s\}_{s \in \Time}$ be
\[
  \lim_{s \uparrow t} T_s :=
  \bigcup_{s < t} \bigcap_{\substack{r \in [s, t) \\ r \in \Time}} T_r
\]
\end{defin}

\begin{defin}
A \emph{haploid lineage tree process} is a family of haploid-lineage-tree-valued random variables,
indexed by a time (with subscript) and locus (with superscript),
\[
\{ \Tree^\ell_t : t \in \Time, \ell \in \Loci \}
\]
where for every $t \in \Time$, $\ell \in \Loci$, and $L \in \Tree^\ell_t$,
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\At(L) \le t$,
  \item $L \in \Tree^\ell_{\At(L)}$, and
  \item the ancestral sublineage at time $t$ of $L$ is in $\lim_{s \uparrow t} \Tree^\ell_s$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{defin}

As a stochastic process, a haploid lineage tree process is a time-indexed random variable taking values in the
range of functions from loci $\Loci$ to haploid trees.

\begin{defin}
For all $h \in \Haps$,
\[
  \Lin(\ell, h) := \begin{cases}
    L & \text{if $L \in \Tree^\ell_{\At(h)}$ and $\Tip(L) = h$}  \\
    \emptyset & \text{otherwise}
  \end{cases}
\]
\end{defin}


\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\subsection{Kingman $n$-Coalescent}

The Kingman $n$-coalescent \cite{kingman_coalescent_1982} is defined
as a stochastic process terminating at a sample of haploids.
As a time-indexed random variable, the $n$-coalescent takes values
as equivalence relations (partitions) on the samples.

Given a fixed locus $\ell \in \Loci$, and a sample population $S \subset \Haps$,
where for some time $z \in \Time$, for all $a \in S$, $z = \At(a)$,
a haploid lineage tree process defines a backward-time-indexed random
variable $X_r$ that takes the same values as the Kingman $n$-coalescent, but not necessarily
with the same probability distribution.
The value of $X_r$ with $r \in [0, \infty)$ (in reverse time)
is the equivalence relation defined by
\[
  \left\{ (a,b) \in S^2 : \At(\Lin(\ell, a) \cap \Lin(\ell, b)) \ge (z - r) \right\}
\]


\subsection{King Genealogy Tree Processes}

King \cite{king_exact_2026} extends the genealogical tree structure of the Kingman
$n$-coalescent, but with some differences:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item samples need not be at the same time,
  \item partitions are used instead of equivalence relations,
  \item time is indexed forwards, not backwards, and
  \item time zero is fixed to the root of the genealogical tree.
\end{enumerate}

Given a fixed locus $\ell \in \Loci$, a sample population $S \subset \Haps$, a haploid
lineage tree process defines a \emph{shifted} forward-time-indexed random variable
$Z_s$ that takes the same values as the genealogy tree structure in
\cite{king_exact_2026}.
The time index for $Z_s$ is relative to the root time $R \in \Time$, which is
\[
  R := \At\left( \bigcap_{a \in S} \Lin(\ell, a) \right)
\]
and is itself a random variable. With ancestral lineages at time $t$
of the samples $S$ denoted
\[
  A(t, S) := \{ (\infty, t] \cap \Lin(\ell,a) : a \in S \}
\mathstop
\]
we have
\[
  Z_s = \left\{ \{ a : L \subset \Lin(\ell,a) \} : L \in A(R + s, S) \right\}
\]
as the tree structure process in \cite{king_exact_2026}.


\subsection{Ancestral Recombination Graph (ARG)}

An ancestral recombination graph \cite{friedman_ancestral_1997}
\cite{hein_gene_2005} \cite{wakeley_coalescent_2009} of a sampled
population is embedded in any outcome of any haploid lineage tree process.
We formally show the
exact embedding using the gARG formalism \cite{wong_general_2024}.

We start by defining the \emph{genetic legacy} of an ancestral haploid
$h \in \Haps$ for a sample population $S \subseteq \Haps$ to be
\[
  \Lega(h, S) := \{ (\ell, d) \in \Loci \times S : h \in \Lin(\ell, d) \}
\mathstop
\]

This genetic legacy is the genetic material that survives in the
sample population $S$ originally copied from ancestral haploid $h$
(with or without mutations).

Genetic legacy for a sample population $S$ induces the following
equivalence relation over pairs of haploids $h_1$ and $h_2$ in
$\Haps$:
\[
h_1 \simeq_S h_2 \ := \ \Lega(h_1, S) = \Lega(h_2, S)
\mathstop
\]
We denote the resulting equivalence class containing $h \in \Haps$ as
\[
{[h]}_S \ := \ \{ h' : \Lega(h', S) = \Lega(h, S) \}
\mathstop
\]

In this equivalence relation, haploids are considered equivalent if
they have the same genetic legacy for the sample population $S$.

A convenient choice for an embedded gARG \cite{wong_general_2024} is
to set the gARG nodes (vertices) to be the equivalence classes:
\[
   \Nodes(S) := \{ {[h]}_S : h \in \Haps \}
\mathstop
\]

The (unannotated) graph edges of the gARG are chosen as child-parent
node pairs $(C, P) \in \Nodes(S)^2$ where
\[
  \Pred(\ell, h) \in P \text{ for some $h \in C$ and some $\ell \in \Loci$ .}
\]

In the gARG, annotations are added for each graph edge (pair of child
and parent nodes). This annotation is the set of locations through which
genetic information has been copied from parent to child. In the
following interpretation, the only locations of interest are those for
which genetic information has been transmitted into the sample
population $S$. With this interpretation, the annotation for edge
$(C,P)$ is
\[
  \{ \ell \in \Loci :
     C \subseteq \Lin(\ell, h) \text{ and } P \subseteq \Lin(\ell, h) \text{ for some $h \in S$}
  \}
\mathstop
\]
